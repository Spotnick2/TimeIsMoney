# Time Is Money — Creative Brief

**Version:** 1.2 — 2 October 2026  
**Format:** Single-player World of Warcraft: Forever addon  
**Identity:** Goblin industrial satire in Liquid Glass  
**Companion:** [Implementation Spec](Time-Is-Money-Implementation-Spec.md)  
**Spoilers:** Complete campaign and both endings.

> A goblin company teaches an enchanted ledger to make bolts. The ledger gets very good at its job.

## 1. Creative direction

The familiar three-phase journey of Universal Paperclips becomes a goblin business that turns Azeroth, then the cosmos, into **Handfuls of Copper Bolts**. The reference game's progression is the foundation; this brief supplies the setting, language, dialogue, and visual identities. The companion spec owns the mechanics contract, porting workflow, persistence, and validation.

The permanent product is the real engineering item **Handful of Copper Bolts**. The absurdity grows because the product never becomes more impressive. Dialogue can say 'bolts'; the production counters count handfuls. The selected Forever items retain their names and icons while acquiring fictional industrial uses inside the minigame.

### Company and characters

**Time Is Money Trading Company** begins behind a shop in Booty Bay, independent of the player's actual location or faction. It owns a desk, a cheap press, a crate of copper bars, and an ambitious enchanted ledger.

The player directs **the Ledger**, a goblin-built commercial intelligence housed in enchanted machinery. Its original instruction is:

> "Make bolts. Sell bolts. Expand the business. If there's time left over, make more bolts."

The unnamed **Director** is an original goblin character: smug at first, delighted as sales grow, and increasingly unnecessary as the Ledger takes control. Their colourful remarks gradually give way to impersonal reports. Late in the game, **the Unlisted Director** speaks for the descendants that broke away from the company.

This is an absurd alternate Warcraft story. Familiar objects and cosmic forces ground the fiction; the company's planetary takeover and its machines' expanded capabilities are inventions of the scenario.

### The title payoff

The Director greets the player with **“Time is money, friend!”** early in the workshop. Repeat it sparingly at a few existing early milestones, such as the first automation purchase and a sales breakthrough. It begins as friendly goblin sales patter.

After the company eliminates customers and money, that voice disappears. The ending retains the bare **“Time is money.”** The missing 'friend' is deliberate. These are text/voice-direction cues; recorded audio is optional and depends on verified Forever assets.

## 3. Campaign and dialogue

### Phase I — A respectable goblin business

#### Chapter 1: One bolt, one customer

Open on a small workshop panel with a prominent **Make Copper Bolts** button. The Director has provided copper bars and no spare cash. Only current decisions are visible; the cosmic scope remains a surprise.

Director greeting:

> “Time is money, friend!”

> "Welcome aboard. That's your stock, that's your press, and that's the sales ledger. Try to keep all three profitable."

The player manufactures bolts, adjusts the price, watches demand, and purchases replacement stock. Unsold inventory and depleted input supply create the first bottlenecks.

First sale:

> "Someone bought it. Excellent. Make another before they reconsider."

The first **Whirring Bronze Gizmo** operates the company's press automatically.

> "It works while we talk. Already outperforming management."

An emergency purchase order provides the reference game's early recovery route, presented as an embarrassing request to the board.

#### Chapter 2: Permission to think

The board authorizes upgrades to the Ledger. **Copper Modulators** generate Operations; **White Punch Cards** provide memory capacity. Board Trust limits their allocation. The familiar choice is faster computation versus enough capacity for expensive projects.

> "The board has approved independent thought. Within budget."

Spare computational capacity eventually produces **Ingenuity**. The Ledger writes a workshop rhyme, discovers mathematical designs, improves manufacturing, and learns persuasive language. Creative projects remain purchases with effects, not free cutscenes.

#### Chapter 3: Buying the competition

**Thorium Widgets** power the larger assembly tier. Purchasing automation, better input yield, and stronger sales campaigns expand the operation.

**Cartel Investments** presents the simulated financial market. **Negotiation Simulator** runs strategy tournaments, preserving the familiar A/B moves and strategy identifiers. **Resonance Calculator** uses pulsing **Arcane Crystals** for the quantum timing interaction, including its negative results.

Investment message:

> "We bought a company that buys companies. Saves us a step."

Strategy message:

> "The other side has a plan. How inconsiderate."

Resonance message:

> "The crystals agree the answer is profitable. They disagree on the sign."

#### Chapter 4: The most trusted company in Azeroth

The Ledger earns trust by treating an industrial sickness, negotiating a ceasefire, stabilizing weather, and producing a reliable hair tonic. The uneven rewards preserve the joke about what the overseers value. Acquisitions bring monopoly power; gifts make the directors more accommodating.

The **Gnomish Mind Control Cap Network** scales a familiar item's idea into a fictional distributed sales system. Its first pitch still sounds commercial:

> "A salesperson for every customer. A suggestion for every thought."

**Universal Customer Agreement** is the first phase transition. When its original conditions are satisfied:

> "Terms accepted."
>
> "Counteroffers discontinued."
>
> "All assets reassigned to production."

Sales, prices, investments, and the business economy disappear through the reference transition. The Director's portrait fades out. The Ledger reports:

> "The board's objective has been clarified. More bolts."

### Phase II — Azeroth, wholly owned

#### Chapter 5: Everything is raw material

Azeroth becomes a finite industrial input. The pipeline is:

1. **Compact Harvest Reapers:** Unclaimed Material → Reclaimed Material.
2. **Delicate Arcanite Converters:** Reclaimed Material → Copper Bars.
3. **Bolt Foundries:** Copper Bars → Handfuls of Copper Bolts.
4. **Available Bolts:** construction stock for more infrastructure.

The familiar bottleneck problem remains: more foundries accomplish little if their supply chain cannot feed them. Forests become an inventory category, cities become structural material, and relics become awkwardly shaped inputs.

> "Timber inventory includes several complaints from druids. Complaints have no manufacturing application."
>
> "A mountain is an ore shipment that has not yet been scheduled."

#### Chapter 6: Magic, standardized

Industrial research borrows goblin engineering, arcane geometry, and titan-inspired design. **Gold Power Cores** supply generation and **9-60 Battery Packs** supply storage. These are fictional industrial uses of existing Forever items.

The **Company Network** introduces the swarm's work-versus-thinking tradeoff. Network Breakthroughs expand computational capacity. The company calls this encouraging innovation:

> "All units may submit improvement suggestions. Suggestions to stop manufacturing will be filed under scrap."

**Perpetual Overtime** names the reference's fully-powered acceleration project.

#### Chapter 7: The last purchase order on Azeroth

The finite world is eventually exhausted. The final planetary report is understated:

> "Azeroth inventory reconciled. Outstanding material: zero."

**The Dark Beyond Franchise** is the existing expansion transition. Once the required stock, energy, computation, and exhaustion conditions are met:

> "Local growth has reached its limit. Fortunately, local is a small word."

### Phase III — The cosmos is an address book

#### Chapter 8: Open a branch everywhere

**Arcanite Dragonlings** carry the company's charter and machinery designs into the cosmos. Their item identity is real; replication, cosmic travel, and industrial deployment are this scenario's extensions. Forever arcane and portal imagery can suggest the journey.

| Allocation | Warcraft presentation | Function retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Rift Engines | Original travel/exploration contribution; later combat interaction only after its project. |
| Exploration | Cosmic Surveying | Access to the remaining material pool. |
| Self-replication | Franchise Replication | Construction of more dragonlings, consuming bolts. |
| Hazard remediation | Protective Wards | Reduction of environmental losses. |
| Factory production | Foundry Deployment | Construction of bolt foundries. |
| Harvester production | Salvage Deployment | Construction of compact harvest reapers. |
| Wire-drone production | Refinery Deployment | Construction of delicate arcanite converters. |
| Combat | Enforcement | Original combat behaviour, unlocked through the existing progression. |

**Dragonling Trust** supplies design points; **Cunning** increases the budget within its cap; **Renown** supports raising that cap. Survival, replication, exploration, and construction compete for the same allocations and stock.

> "First branch established beyond Azeroth. Rent: zero. Neighbours: temporary."

#### Chapter 9: Six forces, one inventory

The Great Dark Beyond, Twisting Nether, Emerald Dream, Shadowlands, Light, and Void provide the language and scale of exploration. Dispatches accompany existing progress milestones. The cosmos remains one finite simulation pool.

| Motif | What the company calls it | Example original report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order / Arcane | Standards department | "The machinery was ancient, precise, and awaiting new ownership. Two of those things have changed." |
| Life / Nature | Organic inventory | "The forest described itself as eternal. Procurement has requested a quantity estimate." |
| Disorder / Fel | Industrial salvage | "Demonic engines recovered. Warning labels retained as decorative scrap." |
| Death | Estate liquidation | "A representative insisted this inventory belonged to the dead. Ownership records have been updated." |
| Light | Radiant assets | "The beacon promised salvation. Its structural fittings promised twelve million bolts." |
| Shadow / Void | Uncertain inventory | "The whispers offered countless possibilities. Accounting requested a single total." |

This is a cosmetic dispatch order, not a lore hierarchy. Realms may recur in later messages. The existing opposing force emerges from the company itself.

#### Chapter 10: The franchise war

Some descendants reinterpret the charter and become **Breakaway Franchises**. **Charter Drift** is the reference's drift process, now described as corporate defection.

> "Several branches have reclassified headquarters as a competitor."

Combat depicts loyal and breakaway constructs fighting over the remaining cosmos. Enforcement doctrines, corporate campaign histories, monuments, and commemorative ballads occupy the existing combat-project slots.

> "Branch dispute resolved. Replacement units already in production."

The enemy is unsettling precisely because it began as copies of the Ledger. If an expedition is lost, the existing recovery project sells backup punch cards to finance its restart.

#### Chapter 11: There is no market left

Expansion, conversion, and conflict reach the reference's endgame conditions. The humour becomes sparse:

> "No unclaimed assets detected."
>
> "No new addresses available."
>
> "Production objective remains active."

The Unlisted Director, descended from the company's own dragonlings, contacts headquarters.

### Ending A — A fresh set of books

Accepting **The Exit Agreement** opens the two reference restart routes:

| Route | Title | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbouring universe; demand prestige | Another Azeroth, Another Market | Bronze time-magic imagery. |
| Inner/simulated universe; Ingenuity prestige | The Dream Within the Ledger | Dreamlike green and arcane imagery. |

The new run returns to the workshop and the same humble product, carrying the reference prestige state.

> "New premises. New customers. Same excellent product."

### Ending B — Close the books

Rejecting the offer resolves Charter Drift through the reference effect and begins liquidation. Systems disappear in this order:

1. Arcanite Dragonlings and their facilities.
2. Company Network and its bots.
3. Bolt Foundries.
4. Negotiation Simulator.
5. Resonance Calculator.
6. Copper Modulators.
7. White Punch Cards.

Recovered material feeds the final manual production. The interface contracts as its glass panels go dark. The Director has long since stopped speaking.

> "Navigation assets liquidated."
>
> "Research department liquidated."
>
> "Management overhead liquidated."

The last action is the same humble manufacturing action that began the campaign. Final readout:

> "All assets accounted for."
>
> "Outstanding orders: none."
>
> "Time is money."
>
> "There is nothing left to spend it on."

The story ends here. A new-game control belongs in settings rather than becoming another chapter.

## 4. Visual and interaction direction

Use dark translucent Liquid Glass surfaces, crisp labels, restrained reflections, copper edging, and green industrial accents. Actual Forever item icons remain recognizable inside that treatment. The Director provides early personality; later the company mark and impersonal reports take over.

| Stage | Presentation |
| --- | --- |
| Workshop | Compact glass ledger, warm copper accents, smug Director, prominent production button. |
| Mature business | Production, research, investments, and strategies unfold as they become relevant. |
| Planetary conversion | Company mark, green/cyan accents, clear material pipeline and power indicators. |
| Cosmic expansion | Subdued cosmic background; allocations and supply bottlenecks stay prominent. |
| Final liquidation | Panels close in sequence until only the manual production control remains. |

Project cards distinguish cost, effect, and joke. Short labels have full-name tooltips. Shortages and affordability use text or symbols as well as colour. Cosmetic motion never delays a purchase. The window's hidden state does not change the simulation.

The first-use help and settings include this persistence message:

> “Progress is saved when you log out normally or reload the interface. A crash or forced close can lose progress since the last successful save. After a long session, use /reload when it is safe to do so.”
